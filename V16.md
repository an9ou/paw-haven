## Part 1: trick training, redesigned (game, dogs, world B)

### Layout: the dog stays visible
- Training mode opens a slim **training panel**:
  - Desktop: a right-side panel, at most 300 px wide.
  - Phone: a bottom strip, at most 34% of the screen height.
- The scene stays undimmed. The camera eases toward the dog: a slight zoom on desktop, and a centred pan on phone.
- The dog performs IN the scene. Nothing ever covers the dog.

### Training loop (modelled on real positive-reinforcement clicker training)
1. **Pick a trick.** A scrollable chip row shows each trick's hand-signal icon, its stage and its Bond lock.
2. **Cue.** Tap "Sit!" (the trick's command button with its hand-signal icon). The player's hand-signal doodle shows briefly near the dog.
3. **Dog attempts.** About 0.6–1.2 s later the dog does something: either the correct trick pose, or a "wrong" behaviour such as sniffing the ground, scratching an ear, wandering, sitting (when sit isn't the trick), lying down, or staring at you. The dog holds whatever it does for about 1.4 s.
   - **Chance of the correct pose** = base 15% + progress × 70% + lure bonus + personality and mood modifiers, capped at 95%.
4. **Mark and reward.** Tap the big **"Good!"** button (the clicker) while the dog is holding the CORRECT pose. A treat flies to the dog: crunch sound, hearts, and progress goes up.
   - **Timing:** marking within the first 0.7 s gives full progress (+20%); later in the hold gives +12%. "Good!" counts while the pose is held; once the hold ends it is "too late", and the line says "Missed it! Timing is everything."
   - **Marking a wrong behaviour** confuses the dog: −5% progress and a puzzled "?" over the dog. The line says something like "Noodle now thinks 'Sit' means 'scratch'."
   - **Ignoring a wrong behaviour and cueing again** is correct technique and costs nothing. A hint teaches this the first time: "Ignore the wrong stuff, reward the right stuff."
5. **Lure (optional).** The "Lure with treat" button holds a treat over the dog's nose: +35% chance of the correct pose on the next cue, but only half the progress. A tip explains why: "Fade the lure, or they'll only do it for snacks."
6. **Focus meter.**
   - Each cue costs 10 Focus, starting from 100.
   - Below 30 Focus, more wrong behaviours appear. At 0 the dog is done: "Biscuit's brain is full. Session over." Short sessions are realistic.
   - Focus refills at +1 per game-minute, and fully after a nap.
   - Low Energy or Happiness starts Focus lower.
7. **Treats.** Training treats are free tiny bits. Each one gives +1 Hunger, capped at +15 per session, so it's realistic and never punishing.

### Stages
| Stage | Progress | Show-off reliability |
|---|---|---|
| New | 0% | — |
| Getting it | ≥ 30% | — |
| Learned | 100% | 80% |
| Mastered | after 5 successful show-off performances | 98% |

### Personality modifiers
- **Biscuit** (food-motivated): +15% chance when a treat is involved, and loses Focus faster when there are no treats.
- **Mochi** (stubborn): −10% chance, but each success gives +25% progress, because he's proud.
- **Sunny** (eager): +10% chance, and Focus drains slower.
- **Noodle** (curious): more "sniff" distractions, and a +10% bonus for learning the dig-like Bow trick.
- **Frost** (chatty): Speak is learned instantly to 60%, but more howl distractions.
- **Pepper** (gentle): a steady +5% chance, and never gets confused by a wrong mark.
- **Rescues:** use their breed personality.

### Show-off mode
- A second tab in the panel. Learned tricks can be performed on cue.
- Chain 3 or more learned tricks without a miss to get a combo: +coins (Learned 3 each, Mastered 5 each, at most 10 shows a day count for coins), plus Happiness and Bond.
- An NPC audience appears in public places (park, Town Square, Dog Park) with a doubled coin bonus there.

## Part 2: the town (world A, world B, new world C, walk, audio, game)

### Map: "Paw Haven Town" (world A)
- A new, bigger town map: `scene('map', {locked, bonds})` now returns a large town, `viewBox 0 0 2400 1500`. It has a street grid, small houses and trees as filler, a river with bridges, a coast with the pier, and the hills to the north.
- Place icons are SMALLER, roughly 120–180 units each, with plain Caveat captions.
- It keeps every existing place, plus all the new places below. Each place is a `data-area` group with a hit shape.
- The game shows the map at a comfortable zoom where about 60% of the town fits on a laptop screen, with drag-to-pan, scroll-wheel or pinch zoom (0.6×–1.6×), and a "you are here" pin. Opening the map centres it on the current place.
- Locks and Bond tags work as before.
- Also expose `PawArt.MAP_AREAS`, an array of `{id, x, y}` centres, so the game can centre and pan to places.

### World C: a NEW module `world/pawart_world_c.js` (new artist)
- It is a plain IIFE that loads after world A and world B. It wraps the existing functions:
  - `PawArt.scene(name, o)`: if `name` is one of `square`, `cafe`, `dogpark`, `vet`, `salon`, `hilltop`, `pier`, it renders that scene. Any other name delegates to the previous `PawArt.scene`.
  - `PawArt.walkStrip(area, o)`: renders `town`, `hilltop` and `pier`, and delegates everything else.
- **Scenes:**
  - Size 1000×600, `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid slice"`.
  - Zones are the same as the hubs: dog area x 300–560 with the ground at y≈500, bowl spot x≈250 y≈520, right decor zone x 620–860 y 330–520.
  - Every time and weather variant. No falling particles.
  - Detailed G doodle style, matching world A exactly: the same pencil colour, paper, palette and stroke helpers. Copy world A's helpers.
- **Hotspots inside scenes:**
  - square: `[data-hot="notice"]` on the board, and the fountain as decor
  - cafe: `[data-hot="cafe-menu"]` on the counter
  - vet: `[data-hot="vet-desk"]`
  - salon: `[data-hot="salon-chair"]`
- Cache everything. No filters. About 30 ms or less per fresh render.
