# Content strategy review (26 Oct 2026, owner's 5 questions)

**What this is based on.** Everything Marketing has made and the captions in `docs/marketing/`, the game itself (v2.17), and what the game's audience is likely to care about. **Not yet based on real numbers:** Instagram blocks reading @doodledoggo.app's stats without a login, and no Reel's views or saves are written down anywhere in the repo. Parts 1, 3 and 5 are therefore hypotheses to check. Send the Insights numbers (list at the end) and they get redone with real data.

## Who the audience is

People who:
- like cozy games and dogs;
- play on their phone in short gaps (commute, lunch, bed);
- are tired of games that nag, punish or charge them;
- often love dogs but can't have one right now (rented flat, allergies, long work hours, a dog that passed away);
- live all over the world (the game is in 11 languages).

**Their goals:** a few calm minutes a day, something cute that is theirs to care for, a small daily reward, something funny to share with a friend.

**Their frustrations:**
- streaks that punish a missed day;
- ads, paywalls and in-app purchases;
- big downloads and full phone storage;
- pet games where pets get sick, sad or die;
- "cozy" games that turn into chore lists;
- no time for long games;
- English-only games.

## 1. What we've posted, and the likely pattern behind the best Reel (to confirm with Insights)

**What we've made.** Every piece we made is listed below, but we don't know which ones you posted, so tell me if the list is off.
- **Long showcases:** the 90 s trailer, the 45 s promo, the 60 s Noodle story.
- **Participation Reels:** "your birthday month = your dog" and "stop the video to dress Noodle" (64 Halloween looks).
- **Calm loops:** the 11 s snack ad and the 18 s world tour.
- **Absurd humour:** the 13.7 s party Reel.

**Likely pattern** (common for new accounts in this niche):
- **Participation beats showcase.** Reels where the viewer finds themselves in it ("your birthday month", "stop the video") get replays, comments ("I got the pug!") and sends to friends. Those are the signals Instagram rewards.
- **Short beats long.** Under 15 s tends to win, because the share of the video people actually finish is high. The 45 to 90 s trailers explain the game well, but most people leave before the end.
- **The crude dogs are the hook.** Close-ups of a crayon dog doing something (munching, dressing up) stop the scroll better than wide scenes or menus.
- **No-text calm loops** (the snack ad) work as ads, but get fewer comments than participation Reels.

**What to check:** compare each Reel's average watch time, the share of viewers who watch to the end, shares, saves and follows. The winner is the one with the most shares and follows per 1,000 views, not the most likes.

## 2. Ten ideas from the audience's problems, goals and frustrations

1. **"I can't have a dog, so I adopted this one"** (can't own a dog). Over a renter's lease or an allergy pill, the crayon dog is adopted on the phone. "No landlord can stop this one."
2. **"Missed a day? Nothing happens."** (streak guilt). A dog happily greeting you after 5 days away, with the calendar: missing a day never costs anything.
3. **"From link to puppy in 10 seconds"** (downloads, storage, sign-ups). One real screen recording: tap the link, pick a pup, pet it. No download, no account, no ads.
4. **"3-minute lunch-break routine"** (no time). Feed, pet, grant today's wish, done. A clock in the corner counts down.
5. **"Pet games always make me sad. Not this one."** (pets getting sick or dying). "In Doodle Doggo nothing bad ever happens. The worst thing is walking into a log."
6. **"Is this safe for my dog?"** (dog lovers want to learn). The game won't let you feed grapes or chocolate; a two-line real-world fact each time. Save-worthy.
7. **"Today my dog wished for…"** (a small daily goal). The v2.17 daily wish in the dog's own words; ask "what would yours wish for?"
8. **"Diary of a crayon dog"** (wanting to feel cared for). A dog's own diary page read out ("Today the human came back. Best day of my life. Again.").
9. **"Draw your dog this badly"** (wanting to share). Followers draw their real dog in crude crayon, and we turn the best ones into a post. Community and comments.
10. **"Play it in your language"** (non-English players). One dog line in 11 languages, with a "which language do you play in?" poll.

## 3. Five new ideas in the same format as the best Reel

If the winner is a **participation Reel** (birthday month, stop the video), the format underneath is: *the viewer picks or is assigned something → sees their own result → comments it → sends it to a friend.* Five new versions that don't copy the old ones:
1. **"Your coffee order = your crayon dog"**: espresso = Chihuahua, oat latte = Samoyed… 8 orders, 8 dogs.
2. **"Stop the video to choose your dog's dinner"**: dog-safe foods spin past, with a cheeky "chocolate? the game says NO" moment.
3. **"Your star sign as a crayon dog"**: 12 signs, 12 dogs with a real in-game line each.
4. **"Screenshot to get today's wish"**: wishes flash fast; your screenshot is your dog's wish for the day.
5. **"Pick a door, meet your dog"**: 3 doors and 3 dogs, comment your number; part 2 reveals the most-picked dog.

If the winner turns out to be the **calm loop** or the **absurd party** Reel, ask and I'll redo this list for that format.

## 4. What the audience needs to hear that we aren't posting

- **"Free. No ads. No in-app purchases."** It's true (see `KOFI_INTRO.md`), but almost none of our posts say it plainly. It's the biggest trust signal for this audience.
- **"No download, no account, plays right in your browser."** It's mentioned in passing, but never shown.
- **"Nothing bad ever happens / no guilt."** It's the core promise, and it hasn't been the main message of any post.
- **The person behind it.** One maker building a game daily (the `/about` story, Ko-fi). People follow people; we have no face-to-camera or behind-the-scenes posts.
- **Real dog care value.** The game teaches dog-safe food and welfare (no toxic food, gentle agility for puppies and seniors). That's useful, save-worthy content for dog lovers, and we haven't posted it.
- **"You asked, we built it."** Updates came from feedback (inside the lines, smooth moves). Showing that turns followers into a community.
- **Players and their dogs.** No posts with players' dogs, names or screenshots yet.
- **Whether progress is safe.** Your game saves by itself, and save codes back it up. This fear stops returning players.

## 5. The next five tests

1. **Participation vs showcase:** a "pick your dog" Reel vs a feature showcase of the same length. Measure shares, saves and follows per 1,000 views.
2. **Hook styles in the first second:** "POV: you can't have a dog" text vs a no-text close-up of a crayon dog vs a question ("which one is you?"). Same body, three hooks. Measure 3-second hold rate and average watch time.
3. **Length:** 7 s vs 15 s vs 30 s cuts of one idea. Measure the share of viewers who finish and replays.
4. **Real dog + game:** a real dog watching the phone (the on-hold phone dog ad idea) vs crayon only. Measure reach to non-followers.
5. **Talking maker vs no maker:** one "why I'm making a game where nothing bad happens" post (voice-over or text, no face needed) vs a normal update post. Measure follows and comments.

Run each test for about a week, with one change at a time.

## Connecting the Instagram API

`brand/instagram/insights/ig_insights.js` pulls every post and its insights (views, reach, likes, comments, shares, saves, average watch time for Reels, profile visits and follows where Instagram gives them) into a JSON and a CSV, read-only. It reads a long-lived token from the environment variable `IG_ACCESS_TOKEN` (permissions `instagram_business_basic` and `instagram_business_manage_insights`, from a Meta app with "Instagram API with Instagram Login" on the Professional account). The owner adds the token in the cloud environment settings (never in chat); a new session picks it up. graph.instagram.com is reachable from the sessions.
