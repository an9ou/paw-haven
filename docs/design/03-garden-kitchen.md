## Placement and pacing

Garden & Kitchen ships as v1.3, an estimated 6-week release between v1.2 and v1.5. It needs only the v1.2 clock and weather and the v1.1 Journal, so it does not have to wait for the second dog. Shipping it alone also keeps v1.5 focused on the risky multi-dog save change and the phone layout.

| Depends on | What it uses |
| --- | --- |
| v1.2 clock and weather | Rain waters crops, snow pauses them, sunny periods speed growth and dry the soil, seasons follow the real month |
| v1.2 runner walk and toys | Dish buffs plug into jump timing, walk length and fetch |
| v1.1 Journal and item abilities | New Garden and Recipes tabs; dish buffs reuse the walk ability hooks (extra dig, Nose-o-meter range, treasure rarity) |

| Hands on to | What it gives |
| --- | --- |
| v1.5 Second Dog & Phone | A Family Feast recipe for every dog at home; the garden and kitchen screens get phone layouts |
| v2 Playdates & Sparkle | Homemade Puppy Mash; mixes inherit one parent's garden perk |

**When players reach it.** New players unlock the garden at Bond 2 (about day 1) and the kitchen at Bond 3 (about day 2). Existing players get both the day v1.3 lands. The first harvest comes about 2 days after the first planting, and most players discover their first new recipe by day 4.

The roadmap in the v2 tab now shows v1.2 and v1.3. If the team prefers fewer releases, the fallback is to fold Garden & Kitchen into v1.5, which grows that release from about 8 to about 14 weeks.

## Farming

The veggie patch becomes a garden of 6 plots, expandable to 12, where 12 dog-safe crops grow in real time. Weather does half the work: rain waters everything, sunny periods grow crops faster but dry the soil, and snow pauses growth. Crops never die; a dry or neglected plot just waits.

### Plots and Pip's Sprout Cart

The garden starts with 6 plots in a 3 × 4 grid; the other 6 are fenced off with a hand-painted "Coming soon" sign. Plots 7 to 9 cost 600 coins and plots 10 to 12 cost 1,500.

Seeds come from **Pip's Sprout Cart**, a new stall on Market Street run by Pip, a retired gardener in an enormous straw hat. Pip sells every seed all year, with an "In season" stamp on this month's best picks. Pip also sells Worm Tea fertilizer, watering tools and a scarecrow, and buys crops back.

### Growth, watering and weather

Each plot holds 0 to 3 drops of water. The watering can fills a plot to 3. Every weather period from v1.2 (00–08, 08–16, 16–24) then uses water and grows the crop:

| Weather period | Water used | Growth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloudy | 1 drop | Normal |
| Sunny (day) | 2 drops | 1.5 × faster |
| Sunny at night (clear sky) | 1 drop | Normal |
| Rain | Refills every plot to 3 | Normal |
| Snow | None | Paused (spinach grows at half speed) |

The rule players learn: one watering lasts a cloudy day or half a sunny day, and rain waters everything for you. A plot with 0 drops pauses and the crop droops dramatically, but it never dies. The "days to grow" below assume cloudy weather, 3 periods a day.

Crops have 4 drawn stages: seed, sprout, leafy and ready. A ready crop waits 3 days, then becomes overripe and goes to the compost bin.

### Seasons by real month

Seasons follow the device's real month: spring March to May, summer June to August, autumn September to November, winter December to February. That is the northern-hemisphere default from v1.2; a Southern setting flips them. A crop grows at normal speed in its good seasons and at half speed in the rest. Winter is the slow, cozy season: spinach keeps growing, compost piles up, and a Cold Frame (1,000 coins) keeps 3 plots growing through snow.

### Your dog helps

The dog in the yard is the Garden Buddy, and each personality brings one perk. With several dogs (v1.5), the player picks one Buddy a day; v2 mixes inherit one parent's perk at birth.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Noodle the Dachshund | Curious, loves digging | Digs planting holes instantly; planting takes 1 tap instead of 2. 1 in 10 holes turns up a bonus seed | Dirt flies everywhere, including on the player |
| Biscuit the Corgi | Cheerful, greedy for food | Guards the patch: the squirrel never steals a ready crop | Stands on the fence like a tiny, round security guard |

### Pests, compost and fertilizer

Pests are comedy, never disasters: nothing is ever destroyed.

| Pest | What it does | How to stop it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rascal the raven | On a sunny day, may steal 1 seed from a freshly planted plot; the seed turns up on the next walk as a bonus pickup | A Scarecrow (200 coins), which Rascal wears as a hat stand |
| Captain Fluff the squirrel | Takes 1 item from a ready crop left a full day, and leaves a nutshell "IOU" | Harvest sooner, or have Biscuit as Garden Buddy |
| Slugs | After rain, 1 plot in 4 has a "slug party" and loses 1 growth period | An eggshell ring from the compost bin. The game never uses slug pellets (see Dog-safety rules) |
| Weeds | Pop up about every 2 days | Pull them; each weed is a compost scrap |

**Compost.** The bin takes weeds, overripe crops, eggshells and peels from cooking, and failed Mystery Mush. 10 scraps make 1 Compost after 2 days. Compost on a plot gives +1 quality point and 10% faster growth for its next crop. **Worm Tea** from Pip (25 coins) gives +1 quality point; compost and Worm Tea stack.

### Quality stars and harvests

Every harvested item gets 1 to 3 stars, drawn as wobbly ink stars. Quality points come from 4 things: the crop never went dry, compost or Worm Tea, a good season, and Mochi's inspection.

| Quality points | 1 star | 2 stars | 3 stars |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 70% | 25% | 5% |
| 1 | 55% | 35% | 10% |
| 2 | 40% | 40% | 20% |
| 3 | 25% | 45% | 30% |
| 4 | 15% | 45% | 40% |

Stars multiply the sale price (×1, ×1.5, ×2) and raise a dish's starting quality. Pumpkins and watermelons have a 1 in 50 chance of growing Giant: a Giant counts as 3 and the dog poses on top of it for a photo. The Journal's Garden page counts lifetime harvests per crop, with ink stamps at 10, 50 and 200. Harvesting 50 of every crop earns the title "Green Paws".

## Cooking

The player's house in the Home Yard gets a kitchen where crops and pantry basics become 17 dishes, each cooked in a 30- to 45-second mini-game with no fail state. Dishes are for dogs only and can never be sold, so their value is a buff, not coins. (This is the player's own house, not the Cozy Cottage doghouse.)

### Pantry basics

Kibble Corner adds a Pantry shelf. Every recipe is plain: no salt, no seasoning, no butter.

| Pantry item | Price (Paw Coins) |
| --- | --- |
| Oats | 2 |
| Rice | 3 |
| Egg | 3 |
| Basic Kibble (existing) | 5 |
| Peanut butter, xylitol-free | 6 |
| Chicken, boneless | 8 |
| Salmon, boneless | 12 |

The peanut butter label is a teaching moment: "Xylitol-free. Some peanut butters contain xylitol, which is poisonous to dogs."

### The cooking mini-game

Each recipe uses 1 to 3 short steps. The dog sits by the stove in a begging pose and reacts to every step.

| Step | What the player does | Scoring |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chop | Tap (or press Space) as a crayon knife line crosses 4 to 6 dotted marks on the vegetables | Each chop is perfect, good or wobbly |
| Stir | Hold and circle the pointer or finger round the pot, keeping the pace inside a glowing "cozy" band for 5 seconds | Time spent in the band |
| Bake, simmer or chill | A pencil dial needle sweeps once; tap when it is in the golden zone (25% of the dial for easy dishes, 15% for harder ones) | Distance from the centre of the zone |

The steps give 1 to 3 cooking stars. The dish's stars are the average of the cooking stars and the ingredients' stars, rounded up, so great crops forgive clumsy hands. The worst result is a 1-star "rustic" dish with a joke: "It's... a shape. Biscuit loves it anyway." After a recipe is cooked at 3 stars once, the player knows it by heart and can auto-cook it at 2 stars, skipping the mini-game.

### Buffs and limits

Dishes feed Hunger and Happiness like shop food and add one buff that ties into existing systems: walk energy and length, runner jump timing, treasure luck, digs, fetch, tricks and Bond. Star quality scales the effect: ×1 at 1 star, ×1.25 at 2 stars, ×1.5 at 3 stars.

- One dish buff per dog at a time; a new dish replaces the old buff.
- Up to 2 dishes per dog per day. Real vets suggest treats stay around a tenth of a dog's daily food, so the rest is kibble.
- Only 1 Bond-giving dish counts per dog per day.
- The fridge holds 8 dishes; a dish keeps for 3 real days, then goes to the compost bin.

### Recipe discovery

The Journal gets a Recipes tab. A known recipe shows its full card; an unknown one shows a pencil silhouette with its number of ingredients and a hint, such as "Something orange, something oaty, something eggy. Baked." The player starts knowing 3 recipes and finds the rest 3 ways:

1. **Experimenting.** Put any 2 to 4 ingredients in the pot. A match discovers the recipe. A miss makes Mystery Mush (+15 Hunger) and a nudge: "Close! Two of those belong together."
2. **Recipe cards on walks.** Soggy recipe cards hide in the v1.1 treasure loot tables.
3. **Gifts from Pip.** Selling 10 of a crop earns a recipe that uses it.

## Dog-safety rules

The game never lets the player plant, cook or feed anything toxic to dogs, and every block is a short, kind lesson, like the merle rule in v2. Each rule below matches common veterinary guidance; a vet should still check the final wording before launch.

**Where players meet a block.** These foods are visible so the lesson can happen, but they can never be used:

- Pip's cart has a "People gardens only" rack (onion sets, garlic, a grapevine). Tapping one shows Pip's note instead of a price.
- The kitchen has a greyed-out "People pantry" shelf (chocolate, raisins, coffee, sugar-free gum). Tapping an item shows a paw-print stop sign and the reason.
- Walk junk finds can include an onion or a bunch of grapes. The dog proudly brings it back, and the game sends it to the town compost with a one-line explanation.

Every note is two sentences: what the food does, then something kind to try instead. For example: "Grapes and raisins can hurt a dog's kidneys, even a few. Blueberries are a great swap."

**Never, in any amount**

| Food | Why it is dangerous to dogs | Suggested swap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onion, garlic, leeks, chives, shallots | They damage red blood cells and can cause anaemia, cooked or raw, fresh or powdered | Carrot or green beans |
| Grapes, raisins, currants, sultanas | They can cause sudden kidney failure; there is no known safe amount | Blueberries |
| Chocolate and cocoa | Theobromine and caffeine can cause vomiting, a racing heart and seizures; dark chocolate is worst | Carrot Crunchies |
| Macadamia nuts | Weakness, wobbly legs, vomiting and fever | Xylitol-free peanut butter |
| Xylitol (birch sugar), in sugar-free gum, sweets and some peanut butters | A fast drop in blood sugar and possible liver failure | Apple Rings |
| Avocado | Persin can upset the stomach, and the large pit can block the gut | Cucumber |
| Coffee, tea and other caffeine | A racing heart, restlessness, tremors | Fresh water |
| Alcohol and raw yeast dough | Alcohol poisoning; raw dough also rises in the stomach | Pumpkin Pupcake |

**Safe only when prepared right.** The game prepares these automatically and says so once, the first time.

| Rule | Why | What the game does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apple cores and seeds removed | Seeds contain compounds that release cyanide, and cores can cause choking | Apple Rings are always cored |
| Watermelon seeds and rind removed | They can block the gut | Slush uses flesh only |
| Sweet potato and pumpkin cooked, plain | Cooked is gentler on the stomach; spiced pumpkin pie filling is not plain pumpkin | Every recipe cooks them |
| No salt, seasoning or butter | Dogs need far less salt than people, and rich fats can upset the stomach | The kitchen has no salt shaker |
| No cooked bones | Cooked bones splinter | Chicken and salmon are always boneless |
| Spinach in small amounts | It is high in oxalates | Recipes use 1 spinach |
| Treats are extras | Treats should stay around a tenth of a dog's daily food | 2 dishes per dog per day |

**Garden safety**

| Never in the garden | Why | What the game uses instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slug pellets (metaldehyde) | Highly poisonous to dogs, even a small amount | Eggshell rings and the dog on patrol |
| Cocoa-shell mulch | It contains the same theobromine as chocolate and smells tempting | Straw mulch |
| Lily of the valley, foxglove, daffodil and tulip bulbs, sago palm | Toxic if chewed or dug up | Sunflowers, roses and snapdragons as decorations |
| Fertilizers with insecticides, bone meal or blood meal left out | Dogs love the smell and can get ill from eating them | Compost and Worm Tea only |

## Economy

A garden earns about 35 coins a day at 6 plots and about 70 at the full 12, if every crop is sold in season. That is a nice top-up to the v1 income of about 250 a day, but still below walks at 100. Cooking spends coins rather than earning them; its payoff is buffs and Bond.

**Why farming cannot become the best money source**

1. Every crop is tuned to about 6 coins of profit per plot per day at average stars, and the garden stops at 12 plots.
2. Dishes can never be sold.
3. Off-season crops grow at half speed and snow pauses most of them, so a year averages well below the in-season peak.
4. Watering is real daily work until the 1,200-coin Sprinkler.
5. Dish buffs that touch coins are small: double outfit compliments is worth about 10 coins a day.

**Garden and kitchen costs**

| --- | --- | --- |
| Seeds | 6 to 40 | One planting (strawberries and blueberries regrow) |
| Apple sapling | 150 | A tree that fruits every 3 days in autumn, forever |
| Worm Tea | 25 | +1 quality point for one crop |
| Watering can | Free | Fills 1 plot per tap |
| Big watering can | 300 | Fills a row of 3 per tap |
| Scarecrow | 200 | Stops Rascal the raven |
| Plots 7 to 9 | 600 | 3 more plots |
| Cold Frame | 1,000 | 3 plots keep growing in snow |

| Plots 10 to 12 | 1,500 | 3 more plots (the maximum) |

The one-time garden sinks total 4,800 coins, about 19 days of v1 income. They land before the v2 sinks (farm barns, portraits), so coins always have a next goal.

**What a dish costs.** Counting crops at their sale price, Chicken & Veggie Rice costs about 26 coins against 15 for the shop's Chicken & Rice Bowl. A Pumpkin Pupcake costs about 50 against 60 for the shop Pupcake. Home cooking is not cheaper; it is better, because it adds Bond and a buff.

## Risks and open questions

The biggest risk is that the garden turns into a chore list that crowds out time with the dog. The design answers with no-death crops, rain that waters for you, a Sprinkler, and a dog at the centre of every garden action.

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Garden becomes a chore | Daily watering could feel like work | Crops never die; rain waters everything; one watering lasts a cloudy day; the Sprinkler automates it |
| Farming becomes the best money source | Players would farm instead of walking | About 6 coins per plot per day, a 12-plot cap, dishes unsellable, slow seasons; check in playtest |
| Bond pacing speeds up | Daily Bond dishes bring Bond 10 from about day 27 to day 21 | Only 1 Bond dish counts per day; the team can lower the Pupcake to +15 if needed |
| Food-safety accuracy | Wrong advice about dog food would be harmful and embarrassing | A vet reviews every safety note before launch; recipes stay plain; a "Real dogs: ask your vet" line sits in the recipe book |
| Players copy recipes for real dogs | Teens may cook these dishes at home | Recipes use real dog-safe ingredients and portions are treat-sized; the recipe book adds "Check with your vet before changing your dog's food" |
| Real-time clock exploits | Changing the device clock skips growth | Cap growth to 1 day per real day on large clock jumps, as the v1.2 weather already depends on the real date |
| Hemisphere mismatch | A southern player sees snow in July | A Seasons setting (Northern or Southern) flips months and snow together |
| Save size | Plots, fridge and harvest counts add data | About 2 KB in total: 12 plots, 8 fridge dishes, 12 harvest counters and 17 recipe flags |
| Phone layout | v1.3 ships before the v1.5 phone version | Design the garden grid and mini-game for touch now; v1.5 only re-lays them out |

**Decisions for the game team**

- Ship Garden & Kitchen as its own v1.3 (proposed), or fold it into v1.5?
- Is the Bond boost from dishes (Bond 10 around day 21 for daily cooks) acceptable?
- Add the Seasons setting (Northern or Southern) to v1.2 now, so snow and seasons agree?
- Who reviews the dog-safety text (a vet advisor), and does it share the v2 welfare review?
- Should the v2 Sunflower Farm add bigger field plots, or stay a place for resting dogs?
- Keep crop sales at Pip's cart, or add a weekly farmers' market day with a small premium?
